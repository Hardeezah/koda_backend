NIGERIA'S AFCFTA TARIFF REDUCTION COMMITMENTS - COMPLETE REFERENCE

========================================================================
SECTION 1: TARIFF PHASE-OUT FRAMEWORK
========================================================================

Nigeria follows the ECOWAS Customs Union timeline as a member of the
Economic Community of West African States. Since ECOWAS includes Least
Developed Countries (LDCs), the union adopted the longer LDC phase-down
schedule.

Three product categories govern the tariff reduction:

CATEGORY A (Non-Sensitive Products) - 90% of tariff lines:
    - Progressive elimination to ZERO tariff
    - LDC phase-out: 13 years from January 1, 2021
    - Annual linear reduction: ~7.7% per year
    - Target completion: December 31, 2033
    - 50% reduction achieved by 2025 (Year 5)

CATEGORY B (Sensitive Products) - 7% of tariff lines (~427 lines):
    - Longer transition with backloaded reductions
    - LDC phase-out: 15 years from January 1, 2021
    - Target completion: December 31, 2035
    - Includes products where Nigeria has significant industrial concerns

CATEGORY C (Excluded Products) - 3% of tariff lines (~184 lines):
    - PERMANENTLY exempt from any tariff reduction
    - Covers food security items, infant industries, fiscal revenue items
    - Must not exceed 10% of intra-African import value

========================================================================
SECTION 3: NIGERIA'S EXCLUSION LIST (CATEGORY C) - ~184 LINES
========================================================================

Products permanently exempt from tariff liberalization:

3.1 FOOD SECURITY PRODUCTS:
    - Rice (semi-milled/wholly milled) - HS Chapter 10
    - Flour (meal and powder) - HS Chapter 11
    - Cocoyam flour - HS Chapter 11
    - Sugar - HS Chapter 17
    Rationale: Protection of domestic agricultural production and
    food security

3.2 INFANT INDUSTRY PROTECTION:
    - Cement - HS Chapter 25
    - Automobiles (certain categories) - HS Chapter 87
    Rationale: Protecting nascent domestic manufacturing

3.3 EXISTING IMPORT PROHIBITION LIST:
    - Approximately 131 products already on Nigeria's import
      prohibition list under the CET
    These are automatically excluded from AfCFTA tariff concessions

3.4 ADDITIONAL EXCLUSIONS:
    - Selected products for national policy reasons
    - Products on the sensitive list with high trade volume impact

========================================================================
SECTION 4: NIGERIA'S SENSITIVE LIST (CATEGORY B) - ~427 LINES
========================================================================

Products with delayed liberalization (15-year transition):

Selection criteria:
- Products where Nigerian industries have significant competitive concerns
- Manufacturing goods facing import surge risk
- Products identified by NOTN (National Office for Technology
  Acquisition and Promotion) in consultation with industry
- Products with high employment multiplier effects

General categories expected on the sensitive list:
- Processed food products with local manufacturing
- Textile products (subject to ongoing RoO negotiation)
- Consumer electronics assembly
- Basic chemicals and pharmaceuticals
- Plastic and rubber products
- Paper and packaging products

Note: The full product-by-product list has not been completely
published but is contained in the gazetted PSTCs.

========================================================================
SECTION 5: COMMON EXTERNAL TARIFF (CET) DUTY BANDS
========================================================================

Nigeria applies the ECOWAS CET 2022-2026 framework:

Band 0 - 0% Duty:
    Essential social goods
    Examples: Medicines, capital equipment, industrial machinery,
    agricultural implements, books, educational materials, medical devices

Band 1 - 5% Duty:
    Raw materials and primary inputs
    Examples: Crude oil, unprocessed agricultural products, basic
    chemicals, minerals

Band 2 - 10% Duty:
    Intermediate and semi-processed goods
    Examples: Processed food ingredients, basic manufactured components,
    semi-finished metals

Band 3 - 20% Duty:
    Finished consumer goods
    Examples: Electronics, household items, processed foods, furniture

Band 4 - 35% Duty:
    Sensitive/protected sectors
    Examples: Textiles, certain automobiles, luxury goods, tobacco

ADDITIONAL LEVIES (cumulative on top of base duty):
    VAT: 7.5% on (CIF + duty + levies)
    CISS (Comprehensive Import Supervision Scheme): 1% of FOB
    ETLS Levy: 0.5% of FOB
    Surcharge: 7% of duty amount
    NAC Levy (vehicles): 15% used, 20% new
    Green Tax (2026): 2% on vehicles >= 2,000cc
    NAFDAC Levy: Variable by product category

Effective total duty burden often exceeds 50% of CIF value.

SECTION 6: TARIFF RATES BY HS CHAPTER (KEY CATEGORIES)
========================================================================

AGRICULTURE (Chapters 1-24):
    Most agricultural products: Band 1-2 (5-10%)
    Rice (1006): Excluded from liberalization (Category C)
    Sugar (1701-1702): Excluded from liberalization
    Processed foods: Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    Live animals (01): Band 1 (5%)
    Meat products (02): Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    Fish (03): Band 2 (10%)
    Dairy (04): Band 2 (10%)
    Vegetables/frruits (07-08): Band 1-2 (5-10%)
    Cereals (10-11): Mixed - some excluded

MINERALS AND FUELS (Chapters 25-27):
    Salt, sulphur (25): Band 1 (5%)
    Cement (2523): Excluded from liberalization
    Ores (26): Band 1 (5%)
    Crude petroleum (2709): Band 0-1 (0-5%)
    Refined petroleum (2710): Band 2 (10%)
    LNG (2711): Band 0-1 (0-5%)
    Bitumen (2713): Band 1 (5%)

CHEMICALS (Chapters 28-38):
    Basic chemicals: Band 1-2 (5-10%)
    Pharmaceuticals (30): Band 0-1 (0-5%) for essential medicines
    Cosmetics (33): Band 3 (20%)
    Plastics (39): Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    Rubber (40): Band 2 (10%)

TEXTILES (Chapters 50-63):
    Rules of origin still under negotiation
    Cotton yarn (52): Band 2 (10%)
    Woven fabrics (52-53): Band 3 (20%)
    Clothing (61-62): Band 3-4 (20-35%)
    Subject to double transformation RoO when finalized

FOOTWEAR (Chapters 64-67):
    Band 3 (20%)

BASE METALS (Chapters 72-83):
    Iron and steel (72-73): Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    Aluminum (76): Band 2 (10%)
    Articles of metal (82-83): Band 3 (20%)

MACHINERY (Chapters 84-85):
    Industrial machinery (84): Band 0-1 (0-5%)
    Electrical equipment (85): Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    Electronics: Band 3 (20%)

VEHICLES (Chapter 87):
    Rules of origin still under negotiation
    Motor vehicles: Band 3-4 (20-35%)
    Parts and accessories: Band 2-3 (10-20%)
    NAC Levy adds 15-20% on top

SECTION 7: KEY EXPORT OPPORTUNITIES UNDER AFCFTA
========================================================================

Products where Nigeria currently faces high tariffs in African markets
and would benefit from AfCFTA zero-tariff access:

HIGH OPPORTUNITY PRODUCTS:
    1. Crustaceans/shrimp (HS 03): Nigeria is Africa's 2nd largest
       producer. Currently faces 20%+ tariffs in key markets.
    2. Beer (HS 22): Faces 30% tariff in Cameroon
    3. Cocoa beans and products (HS 18): Strong export potential
    4. Sesame seeds (HS 12): Already exported to Asia, African market
       untapped
    5. Shea nuts/products (HS 12): West African production advantage
    6. Cassava products: Regional trade potential
    7. Cotton (Chapter 52): Nigeria has production capacity
    8. Palm oil products: Competitive pricing
    9. Fish products: Strong aquaculture sector
    10. Processed foods: Growing African middle class demand

CURRENT EXPORT PROFILE:
    - ~82% crude oil
    - ~4.2% natural gas
    - ~13.8% non-oil exports
    AfCFTA could help diversify beyond oil dependence.

========================================================================
SECTION 8: AFCFTA CERTIFICATE OF ORIGIN (NIGERIA)
========================================================================

Issuing Authority: Nigeria Customs Service (NCS)
Application Portal: Nigerian Export Promotion Council (NEPC)
Processing Time: 2-5 working days
Validity: 12 months from date of issue
Languages: English (AU official language)

Requirements for application:
    1. Valid NEPC registration
    2. CAC incorporation certificate
    3. Product description and HS code
    4. Evidence of originating status (manufacturing records, etc.)
    5. Supporting documentation as required by NCS

The AfCFTA Certificate of Origin is distinct from general export
certificates and is mandatory for claiming preferential tariff
treatment in importing State Parties.

========================================================================
SECTION 9: NON-TARIFF BARRIERS AND IMPLEMENTATION
========================================================================

9.1 Nigeria's first AfCFTA shipment occurred in July 2024.

9.2 FMITI launched a dedicated Exports Air Cargo Corridor to East
    and Southern Africa through partnership with Uganda Airlines and
    UNDP, offering 50-75% cheaper shipping rates.

9.3 The B'Odogwu trade portal (bodogwu.customs.gov.ng) is the primary
    platform for all trade documentation.

9.4 All customs processing is through NICIS II (Nigeria Integrated
    Customs Information System).

9.5 The 131 products on Nigeria's existing import prohibition list
    remain restricted regardless of AfCFTA commitments.
