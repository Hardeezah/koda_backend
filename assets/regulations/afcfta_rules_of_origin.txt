========================================================================
AFRICAN CONTINENTAL FREE TRADE AREA (AfCFTA)
RULES OF ORIGIN - COMPREHENSIVE GUIDE
========================================================================

The Rules of Origin (RoO) determine whether a product qualifies as
originating in an AfCFTA State Party and is therefore eligible for
preferential tariff treatment (reduced or zero tariffs) under the
AfCFTA Agreement.

========================================================================
ARTICLE 1: DEFINITIONS
========================================================================

"Originating goods" means goods that qualify as originating in one or
more State Parties under these Rules.

"Non-originating materials" means materials that do not qualify as
originating in any State Party.

"State Party" means a member state of the AfCFTA.

"Ex-works price" means the price paid for the product ex-works in the
territory of the exporting State Party, after deducting all taxes
rebated upon export.

"Value of non-originating materials (VNOM)" means the value of all
materials used in the production of a good that do not qualify as
originating.

========================================================================
ARTICLE 4: CRITERIA FOR ORIGINATING GOODS
========================================================================

A product shall be considered as originating in a State Party if it
satisfies ONE of the following conditions:

(a) WHOLLY OBTAINED
    The product is wholly obtained or produced entirely in one or more
    State Parties. This includes:
    - Plants and plant products grown, harvested, picked, or gathered
    - Animals born, raised, and kept in State Party territory
    - Products from live animals (slaughtered, dressed, etc.)
    - Products obtained by hunting or fishing
    - Products from aquaculture (fish, shellfish, aquatic plants)
    - Minerals and other naturally occurring substances extracted from
      soil, water, or seabed within State Party territory
    - Waste and scrap resulting from manufacturing within State Party
    - Goods produced exclusively from the above categories

    IMPORTANT: For live animals (Chapter 1), the animal must be born AND
    raised in State Party territory. For fish (Chapter 3), the product
    must be caught in State Party territorial waters or exclusive
    economic zone (EEZ). For minerals (Chapters 25-26), extraction must
    occur within State Party territory.

(b) SUFFICIENTLY WORKED OR PROCESSED
    The product has been sufficiently worked or processed in one or more
    State Parties, meeting one of the following criteria (see Article 6):
    - Value Added Rule
    - Non-Originating Material Content (VNOM) limit
    - Change in Tariff Heading (CTH)
    - Change in Tariff Sub-Heading (CTSH)
    - Specific Process Rule

========================================================================
ARTICLE 6: SUFFICIENT WORKING OR PROCESSING RULES
========================================================================

The following criteria determine whether working or processing is
sufficient to confer origin:

6.1 VALUE ADDED RULE
    The ex-works price of the product, minus the value of all
    non-originating materials, must equal or exceed a specified
    percentage of the ex-works price (typically 40%).

    Formula: (Ex-works price - VNOM) / Ex-works price >= threshold%

6.2 NON-ORIGINATING MATERIAL (VNOM) CONTENT
    The value of non-originating materials must not exceed a specified
    percentage of the ex-works price. The general default is 60% of
    the ex-works price (meaning at least 40% must be originating).

    Default VNOM limit: 60% of ex-works price (for most product
    categories unless Appendix IV specifies otherwise)

6.3 CHANGE IN TARIFF HEADING (CTH)
    The final product must fall under a different 4-digit HS heading
    than all non-originating materials used in its production.

    Example: If you import fabric (HS Chapter 52) and produce clothing
    (HS Chapter 61), the change in heading from 52 to 61 qualifies
    the product as originating.

6.4 CHANGE IN TARIFF SUB-HEADING (CTSH)
    The final product must fall under a different 6-digit HS sub-heading
    than all non-originating materials.

6.5 SPECIFIC PROCESS RULE
    A prescribed technical or manufacturing operation must be performed
    in State Party territory. This is used primarily for chemicals
    (Chapters 28-38) where tariff classification may not change but the
    manufacturing process itself confers origin.

========================================================================
ARTICLE 6A: TOLERANCE RULE (DE MINIMIS)
========================================================================

Up to 15% of the ex-works price in non-originating materials may be
used even when otherwise prohibited by Appendix IV product-specific
rules.

EXCEPTIONS - The tolerance rule does NOT apply to:
- Textiles and clothing (Chapters 50-63)
- Wholly obtained products (where 100% origin is required)

This means for non-textile manufactured goods, even if a product
specific rule requires CTH (change in tariff heading), you can use up
to 15% non-originating materials that don't meet the CTH requirement.

========================================================================
ARTICLE 7: OPERATIONS NOT CONFERRING ORIGIN
========================================================================

The following operations are insufficient to confer origin:

7.1 Preservation during transport and storage (refrigeration, drying,
    freezing, ventilation, etc.)

7.2 Packaging and presentation (breaking bulk, repackaging, placing in
    bottles, bags, boxes, etc.)

7.3 Mere dilution with water or other substances that does not
    materially alter the characteristics of the product

7.4 Combining operations (assembly, joining) that do not amount to
    production of a new or different product

7.5 Agricultural products obtained from products originating from
    food aid, monetisation, or barter arrangements

7.6 Simple mixing or blending that does not produce a product with
    different physical or chemical characteristics

========================================================================
ARTICLE 8: CUMULATION
========================================================================

AfCFTA provides two forms of cumulation:

8.1 BILATERAL CUMULATION (Article 8(2))
    Materials originating in any AfCFTA State Party that are used in
    the production of a good in another State Party shall be considered
    as originating in the State Party where the final production occurs.

    Example: Cocoa beans from Ghana (originating) processed into
    chocolate in Nigeria. The Ghanaian cocoa counts as Nigerian-origin
    input for RoO purposes.

8.2 FULL CUMULATION (Article 8(3))
    Working or processing performed in a State Party on materials from
    another State Party counts towards origin, even if those materials
    do not themselves qualify as originating.

    This means even if raw materials from Country A don't meet the RoO
    independently, any processing done on them in Country B counts as
    originating work.

8.3 PROOF OF ORIGIN FOR CUMULATED MATERIALS
    The originating status of cumulated materials must be proven by:
    - An AfCFTA Certificate of Origin (Appendix I form), OR
    - An Origin Declaration on a commercial document

    These must be issued by the Designated Competent Authority of the
    originating State Party.

========================================================================
ARTICLE 17-31: PROOF OF ORIGIN
========================================================================

CERTIFICATE OF ORIGIN:
- Must be issued by a Designated Competent Authority in the exporting
  State Party (in Nigeria: Nigeria Customs Service)
- Must use the form in Appendix I to Annex 2
- Valid for 12 months from date of issue
- Must be prepared in an AU official language (Arabic, English, French,
  Portuguese, or Swahili)
- Can be issued in hard copy or electronic format

ORIGIN DECLARATION:
- May be used for low-value consignments or by approved exporters
- Placed directly on the invoice or commercial document
- Requires prior approval from the competent authority
- Contains the same information as the Certificate of Origin

ABSORPTION PRINCIPLE:
- Once materials have acquired originating status through sufficient
  processing, they retain that status when used as inputs in downstream
  production
- The downstream producer does not need to re-prove the origin of
  already-originating inputs

========================================================================
APPENDIX IV: PRODUCT-SPECIFIC RULES (KEY CATEGORIES)
========================================================================

The following are the product-specific rules of origin for key HS
chapters. Each specifies the minimum working/processing required.

--- CHAPTERS 1-24: AGRICULTURE AND FOOD ---

Chapter 1 (Live Animals):
    Wholly obtained rule. Animals must be born, raised, and kept
    entirely in State Party territory.

Chapter 2 (Meat):
    Wholly obtained or CTH from Chapter 1. Animals must be slaughtered
    in State Party territory.

Chapter 3 (Fish and Aquatic Products):
    Wholly obtained. Fish must be caught in State Party territorial
    waters or EEZ. Aquaculture products must be farmed in State Parties.

Chapter 4 (Dairy):
    CTH or VNOM threshold. Milk must be collected from animals raised
    in State Parties. Processing into cheese, butter, etc. requires
    change in tariff heading.

Chapter 7-8 (Vegetables and Fruits):
    Fresh produce: wholly obtained rule.
    Prepared/preserved: CTH from fresh state.

Chapter 10-11 (Cereals):
    Wholly obtained (grown and harvested in State Party) or CTH.
    Wheat flour: CTH rule subject to 5-year review.

Chapter 17 (Sugar):
    CTH plus specific process rule. Beet or cane processing must occur
    in State Party territory. Subject to 5-year review.

--- CHAPTERS 25-27: MINERALS AND FUELS ---

Chapter 25 (Salt, Sulphur, Earths, Stone, Cement):
    Manufacture rule: all materials must be wholly obtained.
    Cement: CTH from raw materials (clinker must be produced or processed).

Chapter 26 (Ores, Slag, Ash):
    Manufacture with wholly obtained materials.

Chapter 27 (Petroleum and Energy):
    Crude petroleum (27.09): Specific process rule - refining and/or
    one or more specific processes must occur in State Party territory.
    Petroleum gases/LNG (27.11): Refining/processing RoO.
    Petroleum coke/bitumen (27.13): Manufacture from materials of any
    heading.

--- CHAPTERS 28-38: CHEMICALS AND PLASTICS ---

Appendix IV includes SEVEN specific process rules conferring origin:

    1. Chemical Reaction Origin: A chemical reaction in State Party
       territory that produces a chemically different product.
    2. Purification Origin: Purification process that removes
       impurities and produces a substance of greater purity.
    3. Mixtures and Blends: Deliberate, proportionally controlled
       mixing/blending producing different physical/chemical
       characteristics.
    4. Enantiomer Separation: Separation of optical isomers.
    5. Standards Materials: Production of reference standards.
    6. Isomer Separation: Isolation or separation of isomers.
    7. Biological Origin: Fermentation, extraction, or enzymatic
       processes.

--- CHAPTERS 50-63: TEXTILES AND CLOTHING ---

Chapter Rules:
    Many yarn and fabric headings have agreed chapter rules (e.g.,
    manufacture from yarn).

Double Transformation Rule:
    For clothing (Chapters 61, 62): A double transformation is required
    for a 5-year period with review. This means the fabric must be
    produced (or undergo sufficient processing) AND then sewn into
    garments in State Party territory.

    The tolerance rule (15% de minimis) does NOT apply to textiles
    and clothing.

    Non-originating material content thresholds apply strictly.

    Note: Many yarn and fabric headings (wool, cotton, synthetic staples)
    still lack finalized RoO as of early 2026.

--- CHAPTERS 64-67: FOOTWEAR ---

    CTH or VNOM threshold for most footwear categories.

--- CHAPTERS 72-83: BASE METALS ---

    CTH or value-added rule. Steel products require significant
    transformation (rolling, coating, forming) in State Party territory.

--- CHAPTERS 84-85: MACHINERY AND ELECTRONICS ---

    Generally CTH or VNOM threshold. Assembly alone is not sufficient
    unless it results in a new product with different tariff classification.

--- CHAPTER 87: AUTOMOTIVE ---

    Rules are NOT yet fully agreed for most headings. Passenger vehicles,
    goods transport vehicles, motorcycles, and trailers: rules pending.
    Agreed rules are subject to review after 5 years.

--- CHAPTERS 30: PHARMACEUTICALS ---

    Manufacture from materials of any heading except that of the product.
    CTH rule generally applies.

TARIFF LIBERALIZATION SCHEDULE STRUCTURE
========================================================================

AfCFTA tariff concessions are organized in 3 categories:

Category A (Non-Sensitive Products):
    - 90% of tariff lines
    - Progressive elimination to zero tariff
    - Developing countries: 10-year phase-out from January 1, 2021
    - LDCs (including ECOWAS members): 13-year phase-out

Category B (Sensitive Products):
    - 7% of tariff lines
    - Longer transition period
    - Developing countries: 13 years
    - LDCs: 15 years

Category C (Excluded Products):
    - 3% of tariff lines
    - Permanently exempt from tariff reductions
    - Must not exceed 10% of intra-African import value

Nigeria's Status:
    - 92.3% of tariff lines have finalized RoO
    - Outstanding: most textiles/clothing and automotive headings
    - Nigeria gazetted its Provisional Schedule on April 15, 2025
    - Category A products: 10% annual reduction toward zero
    - Category B (sensitive): ~427 tariff lines with delayed liberalization
    - Category C (excluded): ~184 tariff lines including rice, flour,
      sugar, cement, and import-prohibited goods

========================================================================
KEY TARIFF OPPORTUNITIES FOR NIGERIA
========================================================================

Products where Nigeria faces high tariffs (>20%) in African markets
and would benefit most from AfCFTA tariff reductions:

1. Crustaceans/shrimp (HS 03) - Nigeria is 2nd largest producer in Africa
2. Beer (HS 22) - currently faces 30% tariff in Cameroon
3. Cocoa beans and products (HS 18)
4. Sesame seeds (HS 12)
5. Shea nuts and products (HS 12)
6. Cassava products
7. Cotton (Chapter 52)
8. Palm oil products
9. Fish products
10. Processed foods

Nigeria's current intra-African exports: ~82% crude oil, 4.2% natural gas.
AfCFTA tariff reductions on non-oil goods could help diversify exports.

========================================================================
CERTIFICATE OF ORIGIN ISSUANCE IN NIGERIA
========================================================================

- Issuing Authority: Nigeria Customs Service (NCS)
- Application Portal: NEPC (Nigerian Export Promotion Council)
- Distinct from general export certificates
- Must accompany all AfCFTA preferential tariff claims
- Valid for 12 months from date of issue
- Processing: Typically 2-5 working days

========================================================================
GUIDED TRADE INITIATIVE (GTI) STATUS
========================================================================

The Guided Trade Initiative is the pilot program for AfCFTA
implementation. As of 2024:

- 8+ countries actively trading under agreed rules
- Countries include: Cameroon, Ghana, Kenya, Egypt, Rwanda, Tanzania,
  Tunisia, and others
- Products covered include agricultural goods, textiles, and
  manufactured products
- Provides practical experience for full implementation
