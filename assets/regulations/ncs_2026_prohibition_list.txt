NIGERIA CUSTOMS SERVICE - IMPORT PROHIBITION LIST 2026
Source: Nigeria Customs Service (customs.gov.ng)
Reference: Schedule 4, Common External Tariff (CET) 2022-2026
Revised: April 1, 2026 (Fiscal Policy Measures 2026)
Effective: Full enforcement from July 1, 2026
90-day grace period for pre-existing Form M commitments expired June 2026.

========================================================================
PART A: GOODS PROHIBITED FROM IMPORTATION INTO NIGERIA
(Applicable to imports from non-ECOWAS countries unless otherwise stated)
========================================================================

SECTION 1: FOOD AND AGRICULTURAL PRODUCTS
-----------------------------------------

1.1 Live or Dead Birds, Including Frozen Poultry
    HS Codes: 0105.1100 - 0207.2600
    Covers: Live birds, dressed poultry, frozen chicken, turkey, duck
    Exception: Hatching eggs (0105.9900) are permitted
    Agency: NAFDAC registration required if permitted

1.2 Pork and Beef (fresh, chilled, or frozen)
    HS Codes: 0201.1000 - 0210.2000
    Covers: All forms of pork and beef including offal
    Exception: Processed pork/beef products may be permitted with NAFDAC approval

1.3 Birds' Eggs (excluding hatching eggs)
    HS Code: 0407.0000
    Covers: All poultry eggs for human consumption
    Exception: Hatching eggs for poultry farming

1.4 Refined Vegetable Oils and Fats
    HS Codes: 1507.1000 - 1516.2000
    Covers: Refined palm oil, soybean oil, groundnut oil, sunflower oil (retail packs)
    Exception: Crude vegetable oils (not refined) are NOT banned
    Exception: Linseed oil, castor oil, olive oil are permitted
    Note: Bulk crude vegetable oils for industrial use may be permitted

1.5 Cane or Beet Sugar (retail packs with flavouring or colouring)
    HS Codes: 1701.91 - 1701.99
    Covers: Flavoured or coloured sugar products in retail packaging
    Exception: Industrial sugar in bulk

1.6 Cocoa Butter, Powder, and Cakes
    HS Codes: 1802.00 - 1806.20
    Covers: Cocoa butter, cocoa powder, cocoa cakes, chocolate products
    Note: Cocoa beans (1801.00) are NOT banned

1.7 Spaghetti and Noodles
    HS Codes: 1902.1100 - 1902.30.0000
    Covers: All pasta products including spaghetti, macaroni, noodles
    Note: Local production capacity cited as reason for ban

1.8 Tomato Paste and Concentrate (retail packs)
    HS Codes: 2002.100000 - 2002.909000
    Covers: Tomato paste, puree, concentrate in retail packaging
    Exception: Bulk tomato concentrate for industrial processing

1.9 Fruit Juice (retail packs)
    HS Codes: 2009.11 - 2009.90
    Covers: All fruit juices and vegetable juices in retail packaging
    Exception: Concentrates for industrial use

SECTION 2: BEVERAGES
---------------------

2.1 Waters, Mineral Waters, Aerated Waters, Non-Alcoholic Beverages
    HS Codes: 2201 - 2202
    Covers: Bottled water, soft drinks, fruit drinks, energy drinks
    Exception: Energy drinks and health drinks with specific NAFDAC certification

2.2 Beer and Stout (bottled or canned)
    HS Codes: 2203.0010 - 2203.0090
    Covers: All beer and stout in bottles or cans
    Exception: Bulk beer for industrial processing

========================================================================
PART C: COMMON EXTERNAL TARIFF (CET) DUTY RATES
========================================================================

Nigeria applies the ECOWAS CET 2022-2026 framework with 5 duty bands:

Band 0: 0% duty
    Applies to: Essential social goods, medicines, capital equipment, industrial machinery
    Examples: Medical equipment, agricultural implements, books, educational materials

Band 1: 5% duty
    Applies to: Raw materials, primary inputs
    Examples: Crude oil, unprocessed agricultural products, basic chemicals

Band 2: 10% duty
    Applies to: Intermediate and semi-processed goods
    Examples: Processed food ingredients, basic manufactured components

Band 3: 20% duty
    Applies to: Finished consumer goods
    Examples: Electronics, household items, processed foods

Band 4: 35% duty
    Applies to: Sensitive/protected sectors
    Examples: Textiles, automobiles, certain finished goods

ADDITIONAL CHARGES ON TOP OF BASE DUTY:
- VAT: 7.5% (calculated on CIF + duty + levies)
- CISS (Comprehensive Import Supervision Scheme): 1% of FOB value
- ETLS Levy (ECOWAS Trade Liberalisation Scheme): 0.5% of FOB value
- Surcharge: 7% of duty amount
- NAC Levy (Nigeria Automobile Council): 15% on used vehicles, 20% on new vehicles
- Green Tax (2026): 2% on vehicles with engine capacity 2,000cc and above

Effective total duties often exceed 50% of CIF value on many product categories.

========================================================================
PART D: KEY COMPLIANCE REQUIREMENTS 2026
========================================================================

D.1 All import documentation must be filed electronically through NICIS II
    (Nigeria Integrated Customs Information System). Physical submission
    is no longer accepted.

D.2 Form M is required for all commercial imports exceeding USD 1,000.
    Must be registered with an Authorized Dealer Bank (ADB) before placing
    import orders. Filed via CBN Trade Portal.

D.3 PAAR (Pre-Arrival Assessment Report) is mandatory before goods arrive
    at Nigerian ports. Generated through NCS based on Form M data.

D.4 SONCAP Product Certificate is required for applicable product categories
    before Form M can be opened.

D.5 NAFDAC registration is required for all regulated products (drugs, food,
    cosmetics, chemicals, medical devices, vaccines, narcotics).

D.6 48-hour advance notice to NAFDAC is required before product arrival at port.

D.7 Penalties for importing prohibited goods:
    - Permanent seizure of goods
    - 100-300% fines on evaded duty
    - Up to 5 years post-clearance audit liability
    - Criminal prosecution possible

D.8 The 2026 prohibition revisions apply specifically to non-ECOWAS countries.
    ECOWAS member state goods may still enter under ETLS rules subject to
    Rules of Origin verification.

========================================================================
PART E: CBN FORM M REQUIREMENTS
========================================================================

E.1 Form M is a mandatory electronic import declaration document required
    by the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) and Federal Ministry of Finance
    for all commercial imports exceeding USD 1,000.

E.2 Requirements to open Form M:
    a) Tax Identification Number (TIN) registered with Nigeria Customs
    b) Proforma Invoice (PFI) showing: description of goods, HS code,
       unit price, total cost (valid 3 months from issuance)
    c) Insurance Certificate from a Nigerian insurer covering 110% of
       the Proforma Invoice value
    d) Regulatory Permit: SONCAP Product Certificate or NAFDAC permit
       as applicable to the product category
    e) Completed e-Form M data form

E.3 Form M Validity Periods:
    - General Merchandise: 180 days initial, extendable up to 180
      additional days with bank approval, further extension requires
      CBN approval
    - Plant and Machinery: 365 days initial, extendable up to 365
      additional days with bank approval

E.4 Form M Prefix Codes:
    - BA: Valid for FX (foreign exchange purchased through official CBN channels)
    - CB: Not Valid for FX (importer using own funds or offshore accounts)

E.5 Form M must be processed through a CBN-licensed Authorized Dealer Bank.
    The bank validates all supporting documents and submits to CBN.

E.6 All Form M processing is now done through the B'Odogwu trade portal
    (bodogwu.customs.gov.ng) integrated with NICIS II.

========================================================================
PART CBN FX: CBN FOREIGN EXCHANGE MANUAL 2026 (FOURTH EDITION)
========================================================================

Effective June 1, 2026. First major overhaul since 2018.

Key provisions relevant to trade:

FX.1 All corporate FX transactions must route through the official NFEM
    (Nigerian Foreign Exchange Market) window.

FX.2 Parallel market rates are illegal for corporate accounting and tax
    reporting purposes.

FX.3 Advance Import Payments: Now limited to 30% of FOB value (increased
    from 15% under the 2018 manual).

FX.4 PTA/BTA Disbursement: 75% electronic, 25% cash framework.

FX.5 Tuition Fee Remittances: Up to USD 25,000 per semester.

FX.6 Form NXP (Export Proceeds): Processing fees removed (free of charge).

FX.7 Documentation Violations: Penalty of N100 million plus N10 million
    per affected transaction.

FX.8 Export Proceeds Repatriation: 1% penalty for non-compliance with
    90-day repatriation deadline.

FX.9 eCCI (Electronic Certificate of Capital Importation): Must be
    processed within 24-48 hours of capital entering Nigeria.

FX.10 Domestic transactions between Nigerian entities must be denominated
     in Naira. Exceptions: Oil and Gas, Maritime, Aviation, Free Trade Zones.

========================================================================
PART NXP: CBN FORM NXP - EXPORT DECLARATION
========================================================================

NXP.1 The Nigerian Export Proceeds (NXP) form is mandatory for all
     commercial export transactions from Nigeria.

NXP.2 Requirements:
     a) Domiciliary account (USD/GBP/EUR) with a CBN-licensed Authorized
        Dealer Bank
     b) NEPC (Nigerian Export Promotion Council) registration
     c) CAC (Corporate Affairs Commission) registration
     d) Proforma Invoice
     e) Product-specific certificates (phytosanitary, conformity, etc.)

NXP.3 Export proceeds must be repatriated within 90 days of shipment for
     non-oil exports. Failure triggers NXP flagging, account restrictions,
     and penalties.

NXP.4 Form NXP processing is now free of charge (2026 FX Manual reform).
